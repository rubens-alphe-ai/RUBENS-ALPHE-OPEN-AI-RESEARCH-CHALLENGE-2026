# Handover: Domaine des Trois Chênes, cellar and vineyard operations

The estate farms 18 hectares, of which 11 are Cabernet Franc and 7 Chenin Blanc, on clay and
flint soils. Production averages 62,000 bottles a year. The 2024 harvest yielded 48,000 bottles
after hail in May destroyed roughly a fifth of the crop on the western plots.

Certification is organic since 2019 and conversion to biodynamic was started in 2023; it
requires three consecutive compliant years, so the earliest certification date is 2026. Export
accounts for 41 percent of sales, mainly to Belgium and Japan; the Japanese importer requires
sulphite levels below 60 milligrams per litre.

Two failures are documented. In 2022 a temperature controller in tank 4 drifted by 3 degrees for
two days and the batch lost its aromatic profile; it was sold in bulk at a loss of 11,000 euros.
Controllers are now calibrated before each harvest and tank 4 carries a second probe. In 2023 a
label printing error sent 900 bottles with the wrong vintage to a distributor; all were
recalled, and label proofs now require two signatures.

Rules in force: no irrigation on the western plots, because a 2021 trial showed it lowered
acidity below the level the Japanese importer accepts. Harvest dates are decided from sugar and
acidity measurements taken on three consecutive mornings, never from the calendar. Any tank
above 28 degrees during fermentation must be cooled within four hours.

A move to lighter bottles was proposed in 2025 to cut carbon footprint and shipping cost, but
the Japanese importer has not confirmed acceptance, and no cost comparison has been produced.
Whether the eastern parcel should be replanted after 2027 remains undecided; the vines there are
54 years old and yields have fallen 30 percent in five years.
